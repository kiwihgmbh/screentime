# The rules

The house rules this app was built around. They are a starting point, not a
recommendation. Every family lands somewhere else, and every value in the app is
configurable.

## The budget

Eight hours a week. At most one hour on a school day, at most two hours on
Saturday and Sunday. The daily ceilings add up to more than the week on purpose:
you have to choose how to spend it.

Unused time is gone. It does not move to tomorrow and it does not move to next
week. The point is not to save, it is to decide.

## Looking things up

Fifteen minutes a day, outside the weekly budget, no need to ask.

Looking something up is a question with an answer, and it ends when the answer is
there. Watching a video about the same subject is not looking it up. School work
is separate and does not come out of any of this.

## Evenings

Nothing starts after eight. Sleep matters more than the last round.

## Film nights

On Friday, Saturday and Sunday the family watches a film together. It does not
count. It is a film and not a series, everyone picks it together, and at least
one parent watches. Watching on alone counts again.

There is an end time as well as a start time. A film at eight works, a film at
nine does not.

## The weekly check

Once a week, on Sunday, the log is compared with what the devices report.

A week that matches is a clean week and earns an extra hour the week after, with
a slightly higher weekend ceiling so the extra hour can actually be used. Time
that is missing from the log comes off the following week. Going around the
rules on purpose, with a second account or a changed clock, costs an extra hour.
No week can lose more than two hours, because a week with nothing left to lose
has nothing left to protect.

## Being ill

If you are too ill for school, you are too ill for screens. Your body needs rest
and a screen is not rest. Books, audio books and games at the table instead.

This also means being ill never becomes attractive.

## What is open to discussion

The hours, the evening limit and the fact that there are consequences are set by
the parents.

How the hours are spread across the week, what happens when friends are over,
what the consequence for a broken rule looks like and what gets watched on film
night are all open. Say which is which before you start. A child handles a limit
well. What a child does not handle is being asked and then overruled.
